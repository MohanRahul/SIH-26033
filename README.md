## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
AgriLink AI: A Smart Farmer-to-Consumer Digital Marketplace

AgriLink AI is an integrated digital platform that directly connects farmers, Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), consumers, retailers, hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, and other bulk buyers, eliminating unnecessary intermediaries in the agricultural supply chain. The platform enables farmers to list their produce, receive real-time price recommendations, and sell directly to verified buyers through a transparent marketplace.

The solution incorporates Artificial Intelligence (AI) for demand forecasting, price prediction, and route optimization. Farmers can upload available produce with quantity, location, and harvest date through a mobile application available in regional languages. Consumers and bulk buyers can browse products, place orders, compare prices, and track deliveries in real time.

A dedicated logistics module aggregates orders and connects them with local transport providers. AI-powered route optimization minimizes transportation costs and delivery times while reducing food spoilage. The system also provides quality verification, secure digital payments, and order tracking to build trust among stakeholders.

## Feasibility and Viability
The solution is highly feasible because smartphone adoption among farmers is increasing significantly, while digital payment systems such as UPI have become widely accepted. Existing cloud infrastructure and mapping services enable rapid deployment with minimal hardware requirements.

The platform can initially be launched at a district level and gradually scaled to state and national levels.

Potential Challenges and Risks
Low digital literacy among farmers.
Resistance from traditional intermediaries.
Logistics coordination in remote areas.
Building trust among users.
Product quality verification.
Mitigation Strategies
Voice-assisted multilingual application.
Farmer training programs through FPOs.
Partnerships with local logistics providers.
Quality certification mechanisms.
Escrow-based payment protection.
Government and cooperative support
